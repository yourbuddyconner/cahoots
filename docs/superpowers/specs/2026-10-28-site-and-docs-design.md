# The site and the docs: selling the network, not the badge

*Written 2026-10-28 from a conversation with Conner. The landing page and the docs were built when the badge was the whole idea. The idea has grown: a circle of people who met in person get a set of apps that only work between them, and the card in their wallet is one of several ways to take part. This is the design for the page and the docs that say so.*

## The idea

Cahoots is a private network that only exists between people who have met in person. The page should make a technical reader want to be in one, the way a Bloomberg Terminal page makes a trader want a seat: not by explaining the mechanism, but by showing what members can do that others cannot, and who else is on it. The feeling is "being in cahoots": a place you want to be let into. The flagship is a group chat for your circle, with your card as its key, and the same circle keys your logins, your tunnels, your files and your signatures.

The badge becomes a chapter. The protocol becomes the proof for the people who go looking. The words you read aloud, the thing the old page led with, move below the fold where members use them.

## Audience and tone

Technical people in crypto who have money and taste, who go to conferences and know everyone there, and for whom the card is a status object because of who else carries one. They have read a hundred crypto landing pages and dislike all of them. So: no slogans about freedom, no token, no roadmap-as-promise, no "revolutionary". Plain declarative sentences, a dry wit, numbers where they help, and the look of something expensive and made with care.

The thirty-second line a reader should be able to repeat: "It's a private network that only exists between people who've met in person. You do a five-minute thing in a room, and after that you can message, log in and tunnel to each other with keys no server ever had."

## Goals

- A new visitor understands within one screen what the network is and that it is for people who meet.
- The page names the apps a member gets (chat, SSH logins, WireGuard, sealed files, signatures) as the product, and the ways to take part (phone, laptop, card) as choices with different assurance, with the card as the best one.
- The art direction is one piece across the page, the badge screens and the future app: pixel art of a place, one-bit sigils, the existing dark theme and amber accent.
- The docs are reorganised so a reader can find the story, the system and the hardware without reading internal design notes, and every stale sentence found in the inventory is fixed or cut.
- The page carries nothing that contradicts the docs: six ceremony steps, V1 as the board being built, assurance language that matches `docs/tiers.md`.

## Non-goals

- The phone app itself. The page announces it as coming and links the browser app as what exists today.
- A light theme. Dark only, as now.
- A CMS, a bundler, a framework. Static HTML, CSS, a little JS, as now; the build stays `.github/workflows/pages.yml` and `scripts/build-blog.py`.
- Rewriting `protocol.md`, `chat.md`, `threat-model.md`: they are current. The reorganisation moves and trims; it does not rewrite the specification.
- The blog's design.

## Anti-goals

- Crypto-landing-page clichés: gradients of purple, "trustless", "the future of", token talk, countdowns.
- Leading with the hardware. The exploded 3D badge of the V2 card leaves the top of the page; it depicts a board that is not being built next.
- Claims the system does not make. Nothing on the page may claim more than `docs/threat-model.md` allows; "quantum-safe by construction" stays only where it is true (pad-sealed messages).
- Coined names. The pieces are called what the glossary calls them: circle, ceremony, pad, card (the badge in its wallet form), the feed.

## The page

One file, `site/index.html`, with `site/style.css` extended, `site/main.js` reduced. `site/badge.js` and `site/rig.js` are deleted; three.js leaves the import map.

**Top bar.** Brand, then: What you get, How you get in, Ways in, The card, Docs, Blog, and the button "Open the app".

**Hero: the place.** A pixel-art scene fills the top: a back room late at night, a round table, five people, cards on the table glowing amber, a window with a city, a lamp, a door with light under it. Drawn at a small grid (about 96 by 56 cells) and scaled with crisp edges, so it reads as 8-bit at a glance and as a drawing up close; the palette is sixteen colours built on the theme (near-black, three greys, the amber, a warm white, a cold blue for the window, a green for one screen). It is generated by `site/art/gen.py` from a text map in the script, the way `docs/diagrams/gen.py` draws the diagrams, and committed as `site/assets/place.svg` (rects, no raster) with a `place.png` for Open Graph. The scene also appears at the top of the docs shell at one-third height.

Over it, left-aligned: eyebrow "A private network for people who have met", H1 "Get in cahoots.", lede "A circle of people meets once, for five minutes, in a room. After that they can message, log in and tunnel to each other with keys no server ever had. The card in your wallet holds yours." Two buttons: "Open the app" (the browser app, today) and "How you get in" (anchor).

**Ticker.** The random five-digit groups stay, as a thin band under the hero.

**What you get.** H2 "What members can do." Five cards in a row that wraps: Chat ("A group chat only your circle can read. Keys came from the room, rotate on their own, and live on your card." Coming: the phone app; today: the browser app), Log in ("Your circle is an authorized_keys file. SSH and git signing from the card, with a button press per login."), Tunnel ("WireGuard between members, keyed from the pad. No account, no coordination server."), Seal ("Files and messages sealed to one member or the whole circle. HPKE, age, TLS 1.3 all have a slot for a shared secret; the pad fills it."), Sign ("Approvals, grants and facts signed by a key that only exists in silicon, checkable by anyone against the chip maker's certificate."). Each card has a one-bit pixel icon, 16 by 16, drawn in the same script. A line under the grid: "Today: the browser app does ceremonies, chat, and sealing. The card and the logins are being built in the open."

**How you get in.** H2 "Five minutes, in person." The six steps from `README.md` and `ceremony-guide.md`, in one sentence each, plus the mock e-paper screen as now, redrawn to match the real badge screens (title bar, six words, a footer naming A, A+B and B). The screen's words are the check words, introduced in one line: "Six words appear on every screen. Read them aloud. If they match, you're in."

**Ways in.** H2 "A phone, a laptop, or the card." Three columns, not five tiers: Phone (the browser app; "the phone decides what it protects"; assurance: claimed), Laptop (the host program and the station; a seat for a hosted member or an agent), The card (the badge; "keys in a chip that proves they were made there"; assurance: attested, present). One sentence under them: "A circle is as safe as its least careful member. The ceremony records what each seat brought." Link to `docs/tiers.md` as "How assurance is measured".

**The card.** H2 "A card you keep in your wallet." One paragraph of what V1 is (a 54 by 86 mm board, an e-paper, two buttons, an NXP SE051 that holds the keys and attests them, magnets and contacts so cards stack into a pile) and one of what it shows at rest (a sigil drawn from its own randomness; its contact card with a code). A still image: the front render from `hardware/v1/front.png` on the left and a one-bit mock of the idle screen on the right (an SVG from the art script). Two links: "Build one" (`docs/diy-badge.md`) and "The board" (`hardware/v1/README.md`). The sentence "V2 is the card around the Baochip, designed once V1 has been handled" and nothing more about V2.

**The feed.** As now, shorter: the numbers-station paragraph and the live sample card.

**Open.** H2 "Open hardware, open protocol." Three links: the protocol, the threat model, the repository. The footer keeps the definition of "in cahoots" and the licence line.

**Below the fold is the words.** The hello words and the verification story appear only inside "How you get in" and in the docs; they lead nowhere else.

**Meta.** Title "Cahoots", description "A private network that only exists between people who have met in person. Chat, log in and tunnel to your circle with keys no server ever had.", OG image regenerated from `site/og/home.html` with the place scene behind the headline. The docs and app OG cards get the same scene.

## The docs

`site/docs/docs.js` groups become:

- **Start here**: Overview (README), What you get (`use-cases.md`), Ceremony guide, Glossary.
- **The system**: Protocol v1, Chat, Threat model, Assurance (`tiers.md`, retitled "Device assurance").
- **The card**: Build a badge (`diy-badge.md`), The board (`manufacturing.md`, newly listed), The Pico 2 firmware (`crates/rp2350/README.md`, newly copied as `rp2350.md`).
- **Ideas**: Cards for every gathering, Attested readings.
- **Project**: Roadmap.
- **Reference**: Browser app, The feed, Chat CLI, Simulator, Adapters.

`pages.yml` copies `crates/rp2350/README.md` too. The docs shell gets the scene at the top and a one-line lede under the title: "How the network works, and how to build a card."

**Cuts and fixes, from the inventory:**

- `README.md`: the opening paragraph becomes the new story (three sentences), the mental model stays; the "cahoot (a circle)" aside becomes "a **circle**"; the crate table gains `chip` when that branch lands (the chip plan's Task 11 does that; this design only leaves room).
- `use-cases.md` section 3: the control-message table is replaced by one sentence and a link to `protocol.md` section 4.3.
- `diy-badge.md`: the sentences "V1 has no secure element" and "V1 is T1" become "V1 carries the NXP SE051; see the board's README"; the V2 benchmark paragraph stays.
- `glossary.md`: entries for **card** ("the badge in its wallet form; V1 is a board in a printed case, V2 the card"), **circle** gains "the app calls it a cahoot", **sigil** ("the picture a badge draws from its own randomness and shows at rest"), **contact card**, **attested**.
- `tiers.md`: unchanged here (the chip plan adds the attested rung); the landing page uses its words.
- `site/docs/md/` and `site/docs/diagrams/` stay gitignored; the stale local copies are a local matter.
- `site/badge.js`, `site/rig.js`, the three.js import map, the exploded stepper CSS and JS: deleted.
- `web/app` copy is out of scope except its description line, which becomes the new one-liner.

## Constraints

- No build step beyond what exists: the art script is run by hand and its output committed, like the diagrams.
- Performance: no three.js, no fonts beyond the three already loaded, the hero under 60 kB as SVG.
- Accessibility: the scene has an `alt` sentence; every section is navigable by headings; contrast as the current theme.
- Copy rules from the repo: plain words, no coined names, no em dashes.
- Mobile: the scene crops from the centre, the five cards wrap to one column, the ticker stays.

## Decided after the builder check

- **Order.** The chip branch (`docs/superpowers/plans/2026-10-28-chip-apps.md`) merges first and this work second, but this work must not depend on it: every claim about keys in the chip, attestation and logins from the card carries "being built" on the page, in the same small label style used once under the apps grid. The word "attested" on the page is used only as a plain word with that label; the assurance rung itself is the chip plan's. Conflicts in `README.md` and `glossary.md` at merge time are the merger's job, not this plan's.
- **A sweep, not a list.** The cuts below are the floor. A task sweeps every document the new navigation lists for stale sentences about hardware state (V1 "not reviewed or ordered", V1 "has no secure element", "V1 is T1", "no board at T2", ESP32-S3 as the current chip) and fixes them to today's facts: V1 carries the SE051, five boards are quoted and about to be ordered, the Pico 2 runs the firmware. Hardware facts in `tiers.md` and `diy-badge.md` may be corrected by this sweep; the assurance levels themselves are not touched.
- **"The board" on the docs site is `hardware/v1/README.md`**, copied by `pages.yml` as `v1-board.md`, listed beside `manufacturing.md` ("From breadboard to board"). The page's "The board" link points at the docs route.
- **The sigil is a planned badge feature** (decided by Conner on 2026-10-28; roadmap item M4i, added with this design). The page shows it with the "being built" label; the glossary entry says so.
- **The mock e-paper copies the real words screen** in `crates/badge/src/screens.rs`: the header with the circle name and tier, six words one per row, no footer. Matching the real screen wins over the earlier description.

## Left to the builder

- The exact text map of the scene and the icon set; the brief above and the palette are the constraints.
- The sigil mock's generator: any deterministic one-bit pattern from a seed that looks like a mark, not noise.
- Whether the docs shell's scene is the same SVG cropped by CSS or a second export.

## Decided, and why

- Pixel art generated from code, not a commissioned raster: it stays editable, it matches how the diagrams are made, and it keeps the page small.
- The 3D badge leaves: it shows a board that is not next, and it is the most expensive thing on the page for the least message.
- Five apps named, with "today / being built" said once: the audience will check, and honesty is part of the taste.
- Three ways in rather than five tiers on the landing page: tiers are a measurement, and the measurement lives in the docs.
