# Getting started with Cahoots

Cahoots gives you a private place to keep talking with people you meet in person. You don’t need an account, a phone number, or an address book.

You’ll see two kinds of conversation:

| | Circle | Channel |
|---|---|---|
| What it’s for | A conversation with the people you set up with | Sharing messages with a wider audience |
| How you join | Meet in person and exchange codes | Open a shared link, or get the owner’s approval |
| Who can post | Members of the circle | The owner and people with permission to post |
| Who manages it | Each person keeps their own copy | A circle owns the channel; the device that created it manages it |

Both use end-to-end encryption. Messages are encrypted before they leave your device. A server called a **relay** delivers them without being able to read them. The relay can still see information such as when messages are sent.

## Create your first circle

1. Gather everyone who wants to join. Each person needs Cahoots open on their own device.
2. One person selects **Create a circle**, enters a name, and chooses how many people are joining. Include the person creating it.
3. Choose how to connect. **Through the relay** uses an internet connection. **Codes only** exchanges everything by scanning codes and doesn’t need a network connection during setup.
4. Follow the code exchange on each screen. The person creating the circle is the **host** and uses seat 1. Each other person chooses a different seat number. A **seat** is simply one device’s place in the circle.
5. Compare all six words. One person reads them aloud while everyone checks their own screen. Select **They match** only when all six words match on every device, in the same order.

This setup is called a **ceremony**. If the words differ, stop and start a new ceremony together. Keep Cahoots open until setup is complete.

Once the circle is ready, you can chat from different places. Chat needs an internet connection and a relay that everyone in the circle can reach.

In **People**, add familiar names beside the seat numbers. These names are private labels on your device. Other members don’t see your labels, and a saved name doesn’t verify a person’s identity.

## Check someone on a call with hello words

Hello words help you check that the person you’re talking to can use the secret shared by your two devices during setup. Use them on a phone call, a video call, or in person.

1. Open the same circle on both devices. On iOS, open **People** and select each other’s seat. In the browser, open **Hello words** from Circle tools and select the person.
2. Check the **hello number**. It must match on both screens. If one device is behind, advance only that device until it catches up. Don’t reuse a number from an earlier conversation.
3. Read the pair under **You say aloud**. The other person checks that it matches the reply shown on their screen.
4. Ask them to read their own pair. Check it against the pair under **Listen for their reply**. Don’t tell them the answer you expect to hear.
5. After both pairs match, each of you selects **Next hello** to prepare fresh words for next time. This changes only the device on which it was selected; it doesn’t advance the other person’s device.

If the words don’t match, check the circle, seats, and hello number. If they still differ, stop the check. A familiar voice alone is not enough. The words check access to a shared key; they do not protect you if someone else can use the other person’s device or keys.

## Share beyond your circle with a channel

Select **Start a channel** from your circle and choose who can read and post:

- **Announcements:** anyone with the link can read; only the owner posts.
- **Group chat:** anyone with the link can read and request permission to post.
- **Members post:** anyone with the link can read; the owner chooses who can post.
- **Closed:** only people approved by the owner can read or post. There is no shared link.

The browser also offers **Announcements with approvals**. Posts in that channel need approval from the required number of circle members. Features that need approvals may be unavailable in versions that cannot collect them yet.

Anyone with a shared channel link can forward it. Share it with that in mind. Opening the link lets someone read; posting requires permission. Names on access requests are entered by the person making the request.

In the channel’s menu, **Requests** lets the owner approve reading or posting. **Members** lists people the owner has approved. Removing a member ends their access to future messages and renews the encryption keys. If there is a shared link, removing a member replaces it too. Send the new link to people who still need to read through a link. Other approved members keep their access.

**Disable the link** stops future access through that link. It does not remove approved members. Disabling access cannot take back messages that someone already received or saved.

## Keep your keys and history

Cahoots keeps your keys and history on the device or browser you use. There is no account to restore them from.

- **On iOS:** circles are not included in backups and cannot transfer to another phone. To use a new phone, create a new circle together.
- **In the browser:** clearing site data or changing browsers can mean losing access to your circles. A channel reader can reopen the current channel link, but this does not restore their old local history or posting permission.
- **Removing a circle or channel:** removes it from this device’s app and deletes its saved keys. Other people keep their copies. Browser storage may retain message history or other traces after removal.
- **Removing a channel you own:** also removes the keys this device uses to manage it. Make sure you understand that before removing it.
- **Exporting history:** creates a readable copy of your messages. Anyone with the file can read it. The export does not include your encryption keys.

You can reopen the short guide at any time. On iOS, select **How Cahoots works** in Settings. In the browser, open the home screen’s **More options** menu and select **How Cahoots works**.
