What you get

What your circle can do.

Chat

A group chat only your circle can read. Its keys come from your meeting and stay on your card.

Log in

Your circle is an SSH authorized_keys file. Your card logs you in and signs git commits, one button press each.

Seal

Seal files and messages to one member or the whole circle. HPKE, age and TLS 1.3 take your circle's shared secret.

Sign

Sign approvals and grants with a key that never leaves the chip. Anyone can check it against the chip maker's certificate.

How you get in

Five minutes, in person.

  1. Meet. A group meets in one room with phones or cards.
  2. Draw. Each device makes random bytes and commits to them before it sees the others.
  3. Swap. The devices pass the bytes through a stack of cards or as codes on screen, encrypted so a camera in the room learns nothing.
  4. Mix. Every device combines the bytes into one shared pad, random if even one member was honest.
  5. Sign. Every device signs the list of seats.
  6. Say the words. Everyone reads six words aloud, and matching words mean every device holds the same pad.

The card

A card you keep in your wallet.

V1 is a 54 by 86 mm board with an e-paper screen, two buttons and a coin cell. Its NXP SE051 chip holds the keys and proves it made them. Cards stack face to face for a ceremony.

At rest it shows a sigil drawn from its own randomness, so you know a friend's card across a room. Its second page is a contact card that a phone camera reads.

The feed

Post anywhere. Only your circle can read it.

Like a Cold War numbers station, Cahoots posts to a public feed that anyone can see. Nobody outside your circle can post to it, and nobody can quietly reorder the posts.
