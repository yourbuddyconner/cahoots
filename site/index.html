Keys agreed in a room.

Cahoots is in-person key agreement for the protocols you already run. A group meets once, their badges touch, and a shared pad of random bytes becomes a key budget for WireGuard, encrypted files, group chat, and approvals.

Your browser can't draw the badge in 3D. The parts are listed below.

  1. Polycarbonate shellsCard-sized, four screws, a lanyard slot. Transparent on purpose: the chip's silicon is open and inspectable, so the case is too.
  2. 1.54" e-paperShows the six check words and holds them with no power. Members read them aloud. If every screen matches, every pad matches.
  3. Baochip-1xHardware random numbers with health tests, a key store, one-way counters, and the pad in on-die RRAM, erased byte by byte as it is spent.
  4. Touch linkFour spring pins on one edge, gold pads and magnets on the other. Badges snap into a ring and swap contributions by wire. No radio.
  5. Battery and USB-CA day at the table on a 400 mAh cell. USB-C charges it and carries YubiKey bytes from a laptop before the ceremony, never during.

What it does

The pad is a key budget,
not a message store.

Spend 32 bytes

Seed a session of any size in a protocol you already run. Every one of them has a slot for a secret both sides already share. The slot is almost always empty, because sharing the secret was the hard part. The ceremony fills it.

Attacker must break the math and know the pad

Spend one byte per byte

Encrypt a short message as a true one-time pad with a one-time Poly1305 tag. Approvals, key announcements, the words you say at the start of a call. A 256 KiB pad holds about two thousand of these.

Information-theoretic, as long as no byte is reused

Rotate by pointer

A rotation is a public note that says which bytes come next. Both sides spend the same offset, derive the same key, and erase. No coordination server, no certificate authority, nothing to download.

Every spend is ledgered and erased before the key leaves

The ceremony

Five steps, in a room.

  1. Agree. One member sets the circle name, size, and pad length. Every screen shows them. Everyone confirms.
  2. Commit. Each badge draws random bytes from its hardware generator and shares a hash of them. Nobody can change a contribution after seeing anyone else's.
  3. Reveal. Badges touch in a ring and pass contributions around until everyone holds all of them. Each one is checked against its hash.
  4. Combine. Every badge XORs every contribution into one pad. If even one member was honest, the pad is random.
  5. Say the words. Six words from the pad appear on every screen. Members read them aloud. A mismatch means start over.

Hardware

Concentric rings of security.

Anyone can build a badge from what's on the bench. A circle is as protected as its least protected member, and the ceremony says so before anyone confirms.

T0PhoneThe protocol runs. Erasure is best effort.
T1Any microcontrollerReal erasure in a raw partition. A stolen device is a copy of the pad.
T2SealedSecure boot and flash encryption. A dumped flash is ciphertext.
T3Key storeThe wrapping key never leaves the chip. Bad PINs burn a counter, then the pad.
T4InspectedSilicon compared with the published design under a microscope. The badge we make.

Numbers Station

A public feed only the circle can read.

During the Cold War, shortwave stations read out strings of numbers. Anyone could hear them. Only the agent holding the matching pad page could read them.

Cahoots keeps the idea. Ciphertext goes on an append-only feed. Anyone can read it. Only circle members can decrypt or forge an entry, and that holds against the operator too. The feed orders messages, refuses reuse of pad bytes it can see, and anchors its head so nobody rewrites history.

Four traits make a badge.

  

    
Entropy
A hardware random source with SP 800-90B health tests. The ceremony aborts when a test fails.

    
PadStore
Raw non-volatile memory with an erase unit. Spent bytes are overwritten and read back.

    
Display and Link
Something to show six words on, and a wire to the next badge. The desktop simulator is the reference and the test harness.
